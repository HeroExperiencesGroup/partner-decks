## 1. Purpose

Produce a premium, cinematic pitch deck used by Hero Experiences to propose a formal partnership with National Geographic. The deliverable must convince Nat Geo's travel, licensing, and partnership leadership that Hero is the right operator to co-create the world's definitive desert expedition — and that Hero's full operating range (the Heritage Collection and the Platinum Collection) gives the partnership the access tiers Nat Geo's portfolio already accommodates.

This document is the **single source of truth** for the copy pass (Phase C v5), the per-slide descriptive brief (Phase B0 v5 — new), and the HTML build (Phase B v5). Claude Code should treat this PRD as the spec; all generated artifacts must conform to it.

## 2. Strategic positioning

### 2.1 The one-line pitch

> Hero operates the only desert expeditions in the Gulf where editorial luxury and conservation are the same product — delivered through two collections, both grounded in Bedouin tradition and the UAE's first national park.

### 2.2 The thesis statement (v5 — new)

Conservation travel and luxury travel are commonly sold as opposites. Hero refuses the trade-off. The Heritage Collection delivers documentary, communal, cultural expeditions; the Platinum Collection delivers private, 1:2-staffed, Michelin-curated expeditions. Both operate inside the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve, both follow the same animal-welfare and sustainability rules, both are led by trained guides with conservation responsibilities. **The range is the proof.** Two access levels, one ethos.

National Geographic Traveler covers exactly this kind of operator — Singita, Asilia, Bushcamp Company, the Unique Lodges of the World portfolio — where luxury is expressed through access, scarcity, and expertise, never through material signifiers. Hero fits that profile. v4 of this deck refused luxury framing entirely; that was an over-correction against the *brochure* register, not against luxury itself. v5 restores the full picture.

### 2.3 The defensive frame

This is **not a logo licensing deal.** It is **co-creation of a new category standard** — a structured co-design process to define, validate, and pilot the first National Geographic desert expedition product, extending Nat Geo's travel portfolio into the Arabian desert biome where it currently has no flagship presence. The partnership can scale across both Hero collections (Heritage and Platinum), giving Nat Geo a product family rather than a single offer.

### 2.4 Why this lands for Nat Geo

Hero's operating principles align with the values Nat Geo applies across its travel partnerships:

1. **Conservation** — operates inside the UAE's first national park; gentle-drive-only policy; oryx and gazelle monitoring contributions
2. **Authenticity** — Bedouin storytellers, falconers, and guides are practising members of the tradition, not actors
3. **Sustainability** — solar-powered camps; hydro panels; ecotourism-certified (only operator in Dubai); 200,000 single-use bottles displaced annually
4. **Guest experience** — 1:2 staff-to-guest ratio at Platinum tier; 9-language guide capability; Michelin-starred chef collaboration
5. **Community benefit** — economic return to Bedouin families; cultural education for guests (500,000+ visitors to date)

This values alignment is made visually explicit in the Experience Design Framework slide (Slide 13). We do **not** claim formal eligibility for any specific Nat Geo programme (e.g. Unique Lodges of the World) — that determination is Nat Geo's to make. The deck demonstrates alignment with their values and shows the range of access levels at which they could apply.

### 2.5 Target audience for the deck

- **Primary:** National Geographic travel, licensing, and partnership leadership — across the relevant Nat Geo entities (Nat Geo Partners, Nat Geo Society, licensed travel operators) that hold decision authority on expedition and travel-product partnerships
- **Secondary:** Nat Geo brand custodians evaluating editorial and conservation fit
- **Tone they respond to:** restrained, declarative, evidence-backed, editorial — never salesy; luxury communicated through fact (1:2 ratio, named chef, Royal Family property access), never adjective ("luxurious," "exclusive experience")

## 3. Design direction — Editorial Cinematic, editorial-luxury permitted (locked v5)

### 3.1 The direction in one sentence

The deck looks like an unpublished National Geographic travel feature on a luxury conservation operator — cinematic photography carrying atmosphere, editorial typography carrying information, with disciplined restraint between the two. Think Nat Geo Traveler's Singita feature, not a Dubai brochure.

### 3.2 Why this direction wins for this audience

Nat Geo's travel partnership team sees pitch decks constantly. The decks that convert are the ones that **look like the partner could have made them themselves.** For Nat Geo, that means feeling like an unpublished Nat Geo product — including their luxury-operator coverage — not a pitch *to* Nat Geo.

This rules out three failure modes:
- **Overdesigned tourism aesthetics** (gold-on-black, gloss, drama, ostentation) — reads as Dubai luxury cliché
- **Underdesigned corporate slideware** (Helvetica on white, bar charts) — reads as deal memo
- **Documentary-purity over-correction** (the v4 failure) — under-represents what Hero actually is and leaves higher-up audiences asking where the Platinum tier went

Editorial Cinematic is the register Nat Geo's own coverage occupies, including for luxury operators.

### 3.3 The six rules (was five in v4 — sixth added)

1. **Photography is cinematic, atmospheric, documentary.** Full-bleed where it serves the story. Golden hour, blue hour, weather, and dust as compositional elements. Never lit like advertising.
2. **Typography is editorial.** Serif display for headlines, warm sans for body. Disciplined, never trendy.
3. **Color comes from place, not brand.** Bone, ink, deep sand, warm rust — drawn from desert photography. No gold-on-black. No black-on-gold.
4. **Rhythm alternates by purpose.** Immersive slides carry atmosphere; restrained slides carry information. The contrast is the deck's pacing.
5. **Zero decoration.** No icons, no badges, no gradients, no rounded corners, no drop shadows. Every visual element either carries information or carries atmosphere.
6. **Luxury, when shown, is shown through fact.** A private oasis lit by fire and antique lamps — yes. A chef's name — yes. A staff ratio — yes. Marble, champagne flutes, gold leaf, drone-shot infinity pools — no. The test: would a Nat Geo Traveler photo editor commission this image?

### 3.4 Visual system

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Display type | Serif — `GT Super`, `Tiempos Headline`, or `Söhne Breit` (web fallback: `Playfair Display`, then `Georgia`) |
| Body type | Warm sans — `Söhne`, `Inter`, or `GT America` (web fallback: system sans stack) |
| Meta / caption | Mono or small-caps sans for coordinates, dates, page numbers |
| Color — bone | `#F4EFE6` (primary ivory background) |
| Color — ink | `#1C1C1A` (primary text, dark surfaces, full-bleed dark slides) |
| Color — deep sand | `#B89968` (secondary; section dividers, fine rules) |

| Iconography | Forbidden. Use type or fine line work instead. |
| Logos | Quiet typographic treatment — never badges |
| Page metadata | Page number, date, location coordinates set in small caps in the margin (Nat Geo signature) |

### 3.6 What this direction is NOT

- Not a tourism brochure
- Not a corporate slideware deck (no rounded boxes, no stock icons, no gradients)
- Not a Dubai luxury aesthetic (no gold-on-black, no skyscraper imagery, no ostentation, no champagne-flute hero shots)
- Not a documentary-purity deck either (v4 over-correction) — luxury is part of what Hero is, shown through fact
- Not infographic-driven (data appears as fine typography, not charts)
- Not a Canva default (no rounded cards, soft shadows, illustrated icons, badge-style award marks, template-driven multi-element layouts)
- Not Aesop-style pure restraint (too cool for a story about exploration)
- Not Aman-style pure atmosphere (too marketing for a partnership argument)

**The test:** every slide should be plausibly a page in a Nat Geo travel feature on a conservation-luxury operator (Singita, Bushcamp, Asilia). If a slide doesn't pass that test, it gets restrained until it does.

## 4. Deliverables

Three formats from a single source, plus one new intermediate artifact in v5. HTML build is canonical; PDF and any future Canva/Keynote rebuild are derived.

### 4.1 Phase B0 — Per-slide descriptive briefs (new in v5)

Before any new HTML is written, every slide gets a descriptive brief document covering: what is in the slide (copy + structure), description of imagery (subject, register, lighting, framing), and how it feels (one-paragraph atmosphere note). This is the bridge between PRD and HTML. Output lives in `/natgeo/briefs/` as one Markdown file per slide.

Purpose: catch direction misalignment before it's compiled into HTML. v4 went PRD → copy → HTML, and three slides (6, 6.5, 8) needed structural rework in B2 cleanup because the copy file didn't fully predict the visual register. v5 closes that gap.

### 4.2 Phase C — Copy pass

A complete, build-ready copy document covering all 21 slides. Each section file specifies headline, body, image direction, layout note, and mode (immersive or restrained per Section 3.5). Output lives in `/natgeo/copy/`. Files for new slides will need to be created; files for revised slides (notably 6 / now 8) will need to be rewritten.

### 4.3 Phase B — HTML build (production, hosted)

The HTML deck is the canonical deliverable. Version 4 HTML build (Phase B1 + B2) exists but will need partial rebuild for v5:

- **Stays as-is:** /shared/ design system (tokens, base, components, print, deck.js), fonts, build infrastructure
- **Reworked:** Slide 6 layout (now Slide 8), copy across all revised slides
- **New:** Slides 9 (spec sheet), 10 (two collections intro), 15 (inside the collections), plus the renumbering through the deck

**Technical spec (unchanged from v4):**

- Static site, no backend, no CMS
- Vanilla HTML and CSS; minimal vanilla JavaScript only where needed
- No frameworks
- Single scrolling editorial document with keyboard navigation
- **Mobile-first.** Phone first, then tablet, then 16:9 laptop.
- Self-hosted webfonts (no Google Fonts CDN)
- WebP imagery with JPG fallback; responsive `srcset`; lazy loading below the fold
- Target page weight: cover under 2MB; total deck under 15MB
- Print stylesheet: `Cmd+P → Save as PDF` produces clean PDF
- Analytics: Plausible (lightweight, privacy-respecting, cookieless)

### 4.4 PDF leave-behind (derived)

Generated from the HTML build via browser print-to-PDF. Unchanged from v4.

- One additional cover page at print time with short context paragraph and Hero contact block
- Print stylesheet hides interactive elements; locks layouts to A4 landscape
- File name: `hero-natgeo-partnership-proposal-YYYY-MM.pdf`

### 4.5 Out of scope this iteration

- Video and motion
- Multi-language versions
- CMS-driven content updates
- Public marketing site at the root of the subdomain

## 6. Content rules

These are absolute. Claude Code must enforce them across all generated copy.

### 6.1 Voice

- **Declarative, not persuasive.** "Hero operates the only ecotourism-certified desert safari in Dubai." NOT "Hero is proud to be one of the leading…"
- **Short sentences.** Most under 12 words. No stacked clauses for effect.
- **No marketing adjectives** — banned list refined for v5:
  - **Still banned:** *world-class, breathtaking, unforgettable, bespoke, curated, elevated, journey* (as a noun), *unparalleled, exquisite, immersive* (overused — earn it with imagery), *luxurious, premium*
  - **Permitted in factual constructions only** (new in v5): *exclusive* (e.g. "exclusive access to Royal Family private property" — fact; never "an exclusive experience" — marketing puff); *private* (e.g. "private oasis," "private chef" — fact); *luxury* as a noun naming a product tier (e.g. "the Platinum Collection" / "the operator's luxury tier") but not as adjective ("luxury experience" — banned)
  - **Permitted because verified credentials:** *Michelin-starred*, *Ecotourism-certified*
- **No exclamation points. Ever.**
- **British English** (UAE/Nat Geo convention): *recognised, organisation, programme, centre.*
- **Numerals over words** for any figure ≥ 10.

### 6.2 Headlines

- All headlines sentence case (not Title Case, not ALL CAPS) — except cover labels and section openers, which may use small caps for metadata
- Maximum 8 words per headline
- One headline per slide

### 6.3 Body copy

- 1–3 short paragraphs OR a short list — never both on the same slide (one exception: Slide 15 *Inside the collections* uses paired short paragraphs across columns)
- Lists: 3–6 items (was 3–5 in v4 — Slide 9 spec sheet may run to 6), parallel structure, no terminal punctuation
- Pull quotes: maximum 14 words, set in italic serif, given full visual weight

### 6.4 Credibility claims — source pack

Every claim about Hero must be **verifiable, dated, and sourced.** The canonical source pack lives in `/reference/source-pack.md` and is the only place where approved facts are stored. Claude Code must never use a claim not in the source pack.

Each entry specifies: claim wording; year(s); source; source link; verification status (`verified`, `verified (self-source)`, `pending`, `needs-source`).

### 6.5 Land Rovers and vehicles — revised in v5

The v4 rule (one minimal mention only, no figures, no imagery) is **relaxed** in v5 because both Hero collections have vehicles as part of the current experience product. v4 took a blanket de-emphasis approach to avoid the deck silently committing the vintage fleet to the future partnership product. v5 separates that concern more precisely — surfacing the vintage fleet in current-operations context (where it is genuinely editorial) without that surfacing reading as a forward commitment.

The principle: **the deck is vehicle-agnostic on the forward co-created product.** It neither promises the vintage Land Rover fleet forward, nor states the fleet is excluded from the future product. Vehicle decisions for the co-created expedition stay open by design, to be defined in Phase 1 (Co-design) alongside other operational details. The deck does not take a position on this question in either direction — silence here is the deliberate posture v4 preserved through blanket de-emphasis, and v5 preserves through scope rather than blanket.

In practice this produces the same end-state v4 did on forward slides (no vintage fleet in imagery or copy on Slides 3, 12, 18, 19, 20, 21), while permitting the vintage fleet on current-operations slides where it accurately describes what Hero operates today.

- **Current operations** — what Hero operates today. Heritage Collection uses 1950s Land Rovers described on Hero's own site as "museum-quality"; Platinum Collection uses Land Rover Defenders at a 4-guest cap. These are operating facts the deck may show and describe.
- **Forward partnership** — what the co-created Nat Geo expedition will be. The deck does not name specific vehicle types or depict specific vehicles. The page stays open — neither inclusive nor exclusive.

**v5 vehicle handling rules:**

1. **Current-operations slides — vehicles permitted.** Vehicles may appear in experience photography on slides that describe what Hero operates *today:* Slide 6 (Who Hero is), Slide 10 (Two collections intro), Slide 14 (Conservation/culture/education), and Slide 15 (Inside the collections). Documentary register only — no chrome-and-sky hero shots, no driving-action stills, no glamour signifiers alongside.
2. **The 1950s Land Rover fleet** may be referenced as "museum-quality" or "carefully restored" (Hero's own language) — only in the context of *what Hero operates today.* It is part of the current Heritage Collection product.
3. **Forward-partnership slides — vehicle-agnostic.** Slide 3 (Vision), Slide 12 (Solution), Slide 18 (Implementation), Slide 19 (Big idea), Slide 20 (Ask), and Slide 21 (Closing) describe the co-created expedition. They do not name specific vehicle types and do not depict specific vehicles in imagery. This protects the deck from accidentally promising any particular operational answer before Phase 1 Co-design has run — and equally from accidentally promising against the vintage fleet.
4. **The Platinum Defender / 4-guest spec** may be cited as a concrete current-operations differentiator on Slide 9 and Slide 15. The Defender appearing on those slides describes today; it does not promise the Defender forward.
5. **The "650 cars not produced" sustainability claim** (source pack #32) remains `pending` — do not ship with figure until Hero confirms. If shipped, it attaches to current Heritage operations only.
6. **No CO₂-avoidance figure** in shipped copy unless Hero explicitly approves a verifiable figure with citation.
7. **No vehicle close-ups** (badges, grilles, marque shots) — they are tools of the expedition, not the expedition itself.
8. **No language that promises any specific vehicle forward — in either direction.** Phrases like "your Nat Geo expedition begins in a 1950s Land Rover" are forbidden. Phrases like "the Nat Geo expedition will move beyond the vintage fleet" are equally forbidden. Forward language stays silent on the vehicle question. Phrases like "Hero's Heritage Collection runs in carefully restored 1950s Land Rovers" are permitted on current-operations slides.

**Test for vehicle imagery and copy (two steps):**
- *Step 1 — register:* Would Nat Geo Traveler's photo editor commission this shot for a feature about a conservation-luxury operator? If no, reject.
- *Step 2 — tense:* Is this slide describing what Hero **operates today**, or what the partnership **will deliver**? If the latter, the slide stays vehicle-agnostic (no specific vehicle in image or copy — neither named nor visually committed).
