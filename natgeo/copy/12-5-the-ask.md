# Slide 12.5 — The ask

## Mode

Restrained. Direct, declarative, no ornament. The deck's clearest, most direct slide.

## Headline (sentence case, serif display)

The proposal.

## Body (single declarative statement, set in serif display at full visual weight, centred or left-aligned with generous margins)

> We are asking National Geographic to enter a structured co-design process with Hero Experiences — to define, validate, and pilot the first National Geographic desert expedition product in the UAE.

## Closing line (smaller, set below the body with a clear interval, sans, deep sand or ink)

A pilot. A standard. A new category — co-created.

## Image direction

None. Bone background.

## Layout

Centred or left-aligned (decision deferred to Phase B1 based on rhythm). Generous margins all sides. The body statement is the slide's only large-weight element; the closing line sits beneath, quieter, with a warm-rust hairline rule the width of the body column between them.

Page metadata in small caps in the right margin.

## Notes

- This slide was added in v3 per PRD 12.5 rationale — senior partnership executives need an explicit ask. Without it, the deck implies a partnership but never states what decision is being requested.
- "We are asking" — first person plural is used here, intentionally, because the slide is the moment Hero speaks in its own voice. No other slide uses "we" in this register. The rest of the deck refers to "Hero" or "Hero Experiences Group" in third person. The closing slide (13) re-uses "we" once to close the loop.
- The body names the ask in four verbs: "enter", "define", "validate", "pilot". The four verbs are the structure of the co-design process being proposed.
- "First National Geographic desert expedition product in the UAE" — the slide says "first … in the UAE", not "first … globally", because Nat Geo has expedition products in other deserts (e.g. Atacama, Sahara via partners). The supervisor's read of PRD 2.2 and 2.3 is that the claim is geographic, not global.
- "Programme" is not used on this slide.
- "Co-created" closing matches the "co-design" framing throughout the deck.
- Em dash in "A new category — co-created" is grammatical (apposition).
- No source-pack citation required — the slide makes the ask, not a Hero credibility claim.
