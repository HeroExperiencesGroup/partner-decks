# Slide 6 — The problem

## Mode

Immersive (split layout). Side-by-side photography does the argument. Typography labels, quietly.

## Headline (sentence case, serif display, set across both columns at the top)

Most desert safaris are not desert experiences.

## Body (two parallel columns, equal weight, set under each image — no labels above; the images are the labels)

| Left column | Right column |
|---|---|
| Quad bikes, henna, neon, buffet | Falcon, silence, fire, story |
| Mass tourism | The standard we propose |

Each column carries two stacked lines. First line: four nouns in parallel. Second line: a single descriptor.

## Image direction

Two photographs side by side, equal weight, no copy between except a thin centreline rule.

Left: a commoditised desert-safari scene. A crowded camp with generic strip lighting; quad bikes parked at the edge; a buffet line in the middle distance. Documentary register — not flattering, not satirising. The photograph must read as familiar, not caricatured.

Right: a Hero-register scene. A Bedouin storyteller seated by a low fire at dusk; a falcon on a glove in soft focus; the camp is sparse, the light is from the fire. No vehicles. No quad bikes. No neon.

Both photographs taken at roughly equivalent times of day so the contrast is editorial, not lighting-driven.

WebP with JPG fallback for both. Mobile crop: stack the two photographs vertically rather than side-by-side, preserving the same contrast.

## Layout

50/50 split. A thin centreline rule in deep sand (#B89968) runs the full slide height between the two photographs. The two body lines per column sit in a bone band along the bottom of each image, aligned to the bottom edge of the photographs.

Headline runs across the top of the slide, full width, generous top margin.

Page metadata in small caps in the right margin.

## Notes

- The four-noun parallel structure ("Quad bikes, henna, neon, buffet" / "Falcon, silence, fire, story") is the rhetorical device. The lists are not equivalent in length or cadence — that asymmetry is part of the argument.
- "Henna" and "buffet" name specific, recognisable safari clichés without naming any specific operator.
- "The standard we propose" first introduces the rhetorical claim Hero and Nat Geo are jointly proposing; Slide 7 names that standard explicitly.
- No source-pack citations required — the slide makes no factual claim about Hero's operations.
- No banned adjectives. No marketing register.
- Headline at 7 words; both body lists at 4 items each per PRD 6.3.
