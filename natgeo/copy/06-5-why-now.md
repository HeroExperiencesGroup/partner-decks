# Slide 6.5 — Why now

## Mode

Restrained. Urgency lands in declarative type, not in colour or motion.

## Headline (sentence case, serif display)

This is the window.

## Body (four short lines, stacked, generous spacing, sans body weight, no bullets, no terminal punctuation)

The GCC is becoming the world's fastest-growing premium tourism region

Conservation-led travel is replacing volume-driven travel

Saudi Arabia and the UAE are scaling cultural and natural destinations at unprecedented pace

The category leader will be defined now, not later

## Closing line (smaller, set below the list with generous interval, italic serif)

A definitive desert experience can be claimed once. The moment is open.

## Image direction

Option A (preferred): no image. Bone background only.

Option B (acceptable): a single restrained landscape image as a horizon band at the base of the slide, no more than a fifth of the slide's height. A long dune horizon, low light, no figures. Used only as a quiet ground, never to compete with the type.

Decision deferred to Phase B1 based on how the slide reads after Slide 6 (image-heavy split).

## Layout

Typographic. Left-aligned. Maximum restraint. Headline upper-left, four body lines stacked below with vertical intervals roughly equal to two lines of body text. Closing line lower-left, separated from the body by a warm-rust hairline rule the width of the text column.

If Option B image is used: the horizon band sits at the very bottom of the slide, beneath the closing line, with a clear margin above it.

Page metadata in small caps in the right margin.

## Notes

- The slide makes four assertions about the market. None are Hero credibility claims, so source-pack citation is not required (PRD 6.4 governs Hero claims). The assertions are general market commentary; Jcamp should be ready to back any one of them in a meeting if pressed.
- "Fastest-growing premium tourism region" — a positioning claim, defensible across multiple recent industry reports; no figure is asserted on slide.
- "Unprecedented pace" — editorial register, not numeric.
- The closing line is the slide's rhetorical pivot from "this is the trend" to "this slot fills once." It must sit lower and quieter than the four body lines.
- Italic serif for the closing line matches the pull-quote treatment used on Slide 4.
