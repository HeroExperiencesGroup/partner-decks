# Slide 2 — The paradox

## Mode

Restrained. The idea must land in words. White space dominates; typography carries the argument.

## Headline (sentence case, serif display)

Dubai has a paradox.

## Body (two short stacked statements, generous space between)

One of the world's most visited destinations.

One of the world's most commoditised safari categories.

## Closing line (smaller, set below the body with a generous interval — sans, deep sand or ink)

The gap is the opportunity.

## Image direction

Option A (preferred): no image. Bone background, typography only.

Option B (acceptable): a single sparse photograph in the right third — an empty dune horizon, no foreground subject, no human elements. Used only as a quiet atmospheric note, never to compete with the type.

Decision deferred to Phase B1, where the rhythm coming off the Immersive cover (Slide 1) into Slide 2 can be judged in context. The PRD permits either; the editorial test is whether the slide reads more declaratively with or without imagery.

## Layout

Asymmetric. Type left, image right (if used). White space dominates.

Headline sits upper-left with generous space above. The two body statements stack below the headline, separated by a vertical interval roughly equal to two lines of body text — the space between them is the argument. Closing line sits lower-left, set smaller, in a quieter weight.

Right two-thirds (or right third, if image used) reserved for negative space.

Page metadata in small caps in the right margin, matching the cover.

## Notes

- "Commoditised" is British English (PRD 6.1) — retained.
- The two body statements deliberately use parallel construction ("One of the world's most…") to make the paradox structural, not rhetorical. Editing either statement breaks the device.
- No marketing adjectives present. "Most visited" and "most commoditised" are factual registers, not promotional ones.
- This slide opens the deck's argument; it does not yet name Hero or Nat Geo. That restraint is deliberate.
- Claims here are about Dubai/the safari market generally, not about Hero. No source-pack citation required (source pack governs Hero claims per PRD 6.4).
- "Most visited destinations" — Dubai's high visitor ranking is widely and publicly documented; treated as general factual context, not as a Hero credibility claim.
