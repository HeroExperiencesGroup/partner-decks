# Slide 3 — The vision

## Mode

Immersive. Emotional reframe; the imagery carries it. Typography overlays, quietly.

## Headline (sentence case, serif display, set in bone over the image, bottom-left)

From desert safari to global desert heritage expedition.

## Body (two-line caption format, set in bone, sans body weight, below the headline)

A co-created experience between National Geographic and Hero Experiences Group — the first product to sit alongside Nat Geo's global expedition portfolio in the Arabian desert.

## Image direction

Full-bleed photograph. Wide shot at golden hour. Landscape-dominant; the frame is mostly land and sky. The composition suggests scale and silence — long horizon, soft volumetric light raking across dune fields, no figures.

No vehicles in frame (PRD 6.5). No camp, no built structures, no people. The desert alone.

Treatment: warm, documentary. Underexpose slightly to keep the sky from going chalky. The image must read as an unpublished Nat Geo feature opener, not a hotel campaign.

WebP with JPG fallback. Mobile-first crop preserves the horizon line in the lower third on portrait orientation.

## Layout

Image dominates the slide, edge to edge. Headline sits bottom-left in bone over the image, with enough negative space above to let the landscape breathe. Body sits directly below the headline as a two-line caption — same left alignment, smaller optical size, sans weight.

The headline and body together form a low-left text block; the rest of the frame is photograph.

Page metadata in small caps in the right margin.

## Notes

- The headline is the deck's one-line pitch from PRD 2.1, rendered as a clean editorial sentence (the original "→" arrow rendering is dropped; the typographic version reads as a Nat Geo feature subhead, which is the register we want).
- "Co-created experience" sets the defensive frame from PRD 2.2: this is not licensing, it is co-design. The slide names that frame early so every later slide inherits it.
- "First product to sit alongside Nat Geo's global expedition portfolio in the Arabian desert" is a positioning claim about what this would be, not a claim of formal eligibility for any specific programme (PRD 2.3, alignment checklist).
- The em dash in the body is grammatical, not stylistic — used to apposition the descriptive clause to the noun phrase (PRD 6.1 "use sparingly, grammatically only").
- No source-pack citation required — the body makes no factual claim about Hero's operations; it is a proposal statement.
- "Desert" appears three times across headline and body. Acceptable — the repetition is the device (the slide is *about* desert as category).
