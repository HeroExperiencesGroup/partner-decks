# Slide 14 — Conservation, culture, education

## What's in it

- Headline (serif display, centred or left-aligned, generous top margin): *Three threads run through every expedition.*
- **Three editorial tabs** (hairline-underline pattern, on immersive — colors invert): *Conservation · Culture · Education.* Default active: *Conservation.*

### Tab A — Conservation (default active)

- Label: *Conservation*
- Body: Active partnership with the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve. Guest expeditions contribute directly to oryx and gazelle monitoring programmes. Every visit funds the landscape that hosts it.
- Facts strip: *UAE's first national park · 200,000 single-use bottles displaced annually · Gentle-drive policy · animal-welfare compliant*

### Tab B — Culture

- Label: *Culture*
- Body: Bedouin storytellers, falconers, and guides — not actors. The traditions guests encounter are still practised by the families that practise them. Living heritage, not performance.
- Facts strip: *Bedouin host families · 9-language guide capability · Falconry as practice, not show · Traditions taught, not staged*

### Tab C — Education

- Label: *Education*
- Body: Each expedition framed as field learning. Geology, ecology, navigation, astronomy, anthropology — taught by domain practitioners, not narrators. 500,000+ visitors connected to date.
- Facts strip: *Field learning · domain practitioners · Telescope-led astronomy (Private Night Safari) · 500,000+ visitors connected to date*

## Feel

The deck's editorial heart. v5 ran three threads at once in a magazine grid — strong but every cell competed for space. v5.1 lets each thread occupy the full slide one at a time. Tabs feel like turning pages in a Nat Geo Traveler feature dedicated to *one* thread per spread. Image is dominant; copy is the caption-plus-detail; facts strip is the editorial sidebar. The reader feels conservation, culture, and education as *operating threads*, not marketing pillars — and now has room to feel each one fully.
