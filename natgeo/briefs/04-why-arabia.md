# Slide 4 — Why the Arabian desert matters

## What's in it

- Headline (serif display, overlaid in bone band on lower third): *The desert is older than the city around it.*
- Body — short atmospheric paragraph, sans, set in the bone band below the headline:
  > The Arabian desert holds 12,000 years of human cultural memory and one of the most distinctive arid ecosystems on Earth. The Bedouin tradition that mapped it is still alive. The wildlife that defined it — oryx, gazelle, sand fox — is returning. This landscape predates Dubai by millennia. It is the heritage Dubai is built on top of.

## Image

Full-bleed wide landscape — long horizon, sand and sky, low light (dawn or last light). No human elements. No vehicles. Aspect: cinematic wide. The image carries time-depth — geological scale, not tourist scale. Ghaf scrub or wind-carved sand permitted as foreground texture; signs of human presence forbidden.

## Feel

A deliberate slowing of the deck's pace. Before any commercial argument, the reader is reminded what is at stake. The image is doing the heavy lifting; the copy is caption to a feature photograph. Mood is contemplative, almost reverent — without slipping into mysticism. This is the slide that earns the reader's seriousness for the next four slides of credibility argument.

## Notes

- 12,000-year figure is a long-established orientation point in Arabian peninsula prehistory; ship as written.
