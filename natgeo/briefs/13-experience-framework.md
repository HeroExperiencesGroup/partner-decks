# Slide 13 — Experience design framework

## What's in it

- Headline (serif display, centred or left-aligned, generous top margin): *Five pillars. One expedition. Two access levels.*
- Body — five rows. Each row: pillar name in serif display (left), detail in sans on same line, separated by an em dash. Fine warm-rust hairline rule between rows, full text-column width.
  - **Exploration** — guided by trained Conservation Guides; geology, ecology, navigation; available at both tiers
  - **Wildlife and conservation** — Arabian oryx, gazelle, and desert fox tracking; gentle-drive policy; partnership with DDCR
  - **Cultural heritage** — Bedouin tradition and falconry as living practice, not performance; not actors
  - **Immersion** — silence zones; night-sky astronomy with telescope-led talks; protected reserve
  - **Storytelling** — each expedition framed as documentary; guests as participants, not spectators
- Footer (italic serif, smaller, generous top margin from the last row): *Aligned with the values Nat Geo applies across its travel partnerships: conservation, authenticity, sustainability, guest experience, and community benefit.*

## Image

None. Bone background.

## Feel

The deck's structural argument. Five rows, identical visual rhythm — the framework *is* the slide. The footer makes the values-alignment statement explicit without overplaying it. Reader leaves with a clean mental model: five pillars span both tiers; one operating ethos underneath both.
